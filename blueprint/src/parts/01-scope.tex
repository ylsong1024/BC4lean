\chapter{Scope, conventions, and prerequisites}
\label{ch:scope}

\section{The version of the conjecture}
The main target is the Baum--Connes conjecture for all countable discrete
groups, without coefficients, using the reduced group $C^*$-algebra.
The degree is $i\in\{0,1\}$. All algebras and Hilbert spaces are complex.
Write $\Gamma$ for a variable group; no particular matrix group is built
into the definitions.

\section{Standing assumptions}
The group algebra, the regular representation, and the reduced group
$C^*$-algebra make sense for arbitrary discrete groups. Impose countability
for the main conjecture and the chosen separable KK-theoretic route.
Specify all local compactness, Hausdorffness, and second-countability
assumptions on spaces where they are needed.

\section{Background interfaces}
Organize prerequisites for Hilbert spaces, bounded operators, complex
$C^*$-algebras (including nonunital algebras), functions vanishing at infinity,
gradings, and filtered colimits of abelian groups.

\section{Status conventions}
The blueprint records verified foundations alongside later targets.
Nodes marked as not ready are specifications to expand. Lean verification
covers the reduced group $C^*$-algebra foundations and the operator
$K$-theory constructions used in the comparisons below, including nonunital
algebras and both degrees. Chapter~4 verifies the universal proper-space
construction for countable discrete groups. Chapter~5 verifies the full
even/odd equivariant KK groups, Kasparov product existence and laws, and
natural comparisons with ordinary operator $K_0$ and $K_1$, under the
stated separability and universe hypotheses. Its verification record
documents the source corrections and exact Lean declarations.
Chapter~6 verifies the actual topological $K$-homology colimit, its transition
laws, cofinal indexing and universal proper-model independence, with the stated
local compactness, second-countability and universe hypotheses.
The assembly construction remains a later milestone;
the general Baum--Connes conjecture is not proved here. Separate construction
of the objects, formalization of the conjecture's statement, and proof of
the conjecture.
